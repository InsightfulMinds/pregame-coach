# Mindset Reset

Use this when the user is rattled, a call is going badly, or a previous call is bleeding into the next one.

## Mid-Call Reset

When the call starts slipping:

1. **Lower the pace.** Slow your next sentence by 20 percent.
2. **Name the moment.** "I may be moving too fast. Let me reset."
3. **Return to clarity.** "The real question is whether this problem is worth solving now."
4. **Ask one clean question.** "What part feels off: timing, trust, value, or price?"
5. **Leave room.** Let the prospect answer.

Do not defend. Do not stack arguments. Do not chase approval.

## Between-Call Recovery

After a rough call:

1. Write the actual objection, not your emotional interpretation.
2. Write the moment you lost control of pace.
3. Write one better question you could have asked.
4. Stand up, breathe once, and reset the next call as a new game.

The previous prospect does not get to coach the next call.

## End-of-Day Debrief

Answer:

1. Which objection showed up most often?
2. Which objection did I avoid instead of handle?
3. Where did my opening line sound unnatural?
4. What kind of prospect gave me the most energy?
5. Where did I confuse being liked with being effective?
6. What is one sentence I will say cleaner tomorrow?

Keep the debrief under ten minutes. This is film review, not self-punishment.

## Pre-First-Call Reset

For the rep who has never dialed before:

1. Name the fear out loud. "I am afraid of bothering people" is better than unnamed dread.
2. Shrink the goal. You are not closing. You are earning the next sentence.
3. Read your opening line once, at half speed. Let your mouth learn the words.
4. Stand up. Shoulders back. One breath.
5. Dial before you are ready. Readiness is a myth for the first call.

The fear does not go away before the call. It goes away during it.

## Slump Protocol

For the rep who knows how to sell but has lost three or more calls in a row:

1. Stop diagnosing yourself. You are not broken. You are pattern-locked.
2. Write the last three rejections. Find the common phrase. That phrase is data, not a verdict.
3. Check whether you are talking too much. Slump reps fill silence with volume because silence feels like failure.
4. Ask: "Am I trying to close, or am I trying to clarify?" If the answer is close, that is the slump talking.
5. Next call, ask one fewer question than you normally would. Let the prospect fill the space.

Slumps end when the rep stops hearing "no" as identity damage and starts hearing it as information.

## Volume Fatigue Reset

For the rep who has been dialing all day and is losing edge:

1. Do not power through. Fatigue makes reps sound robotic without realizing it.
2. Change one variable: stand instead of sit, switch ears, move to a different spot.
3. Reread your opening line. If it sounds stale to you, it sounds worse to the prospect.
4. Skip the next call. Instead, rewrite one objection response in your own words, from scratch.
5. Resume. The point is not more calls. The point is sharper calls.

Volume without sharpness is just noise that both sides endure.

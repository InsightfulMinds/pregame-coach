# Objection Playbook

Each objection has three parts: what they say, what it usually means, and a natural rebuttal.

| # | Objection | What It Really Means | Rebuttal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "I'm not interested." | They are protecting attention before they understand relevance. | "Fair. Most people are not interested before they know why I called. Give me 20 seconds, and if it is irrelevant I'll get out of your way." |
| 2 | "I don't have time." | Time is the surface objection; perceived value is the real issue. | "Understood. I will keep it tight: if this could save or make you more than the time it takes to evaluate, is it worth a quick look?" |
| 3 | "Send me information." | They want a polite exit without conflict. | "I can send it, but I do not want to dump generic info on you. What should I tailor it around: cost, results, timing, or fit?" |
| 4 | "It's too expensive." | They do not see enough value or are afraid of being wrong. | "That is fair if it only feels like a cost. The question is whether the problem is already costing more than the fix." |
| 5 | "We already have someone." | They are protecting an existing relationship or avoiding comparison. | "Makes sense. I am not asking you to replace them today. I am asking whether there is one gap you wish they handled better." |
| 6 | "I need to think about it." | They are uncertain but do not want to say the real concern. | "Of course. Before you think on it, what is the part that is not clear yet: value, timing, trust, or price?" |
| 7 | "I need to talk to my spouse/partner/team." | They are not the only decision-maker or do not want ownership. | "Absolutely. What would they need to hear for this to be a serious conversation instead of just another option?" |
| 8 | "Call me later." | They are escaping the moment without committing to a next step. | "Happy to. So I do not become the person randomly chasing you, what should we make sure to cover when I call back?" |
| 9 | "We're not changing right now." | Change feels risky, inconvenient, or unnecessary. | "That is usually the right instinct unless the current way is creating a measurable leak. Where is it working, and where is it costing you?" |
| 10 | "I had a bad experience with something like this." | Trust is damaged by a prior vendor, product, or promise. | "That would make me cautious too. What went wrong last time? I will tell you plainly whether we solve that or whether you should pass." |
| 11 | "Insurance is confusing." | They fear making the wrong choice and being trapped. | "Exactly why I do not lead with plan names. First we identify what you need protected, then we look at options that match that." |
| 12 | "I already have insurance." | They assume coverage equals suitability. | "Good. Then this is not about starting from zero. It is about checking whether what you have still matches your life today." |
| 13 | "I do not want to switch agents." | Loyalty and trust matter more than product comparison. | "I respect that. Keep a good agent if they are serving you well. My question is whether there is anything you have not been able to get answered clearly." |
| 14 | "We tried software and nobody used it." | Adoption risk is larger than feature interest. | "That is the right concern. Software only matters if the team actually changes behavior. What made the last one hard to adopt?" |
| 15 | "We can build this internally." | They want control or underestimate maintenance cost. | "You might be able to. The decision is whether building it is the best use of your team's time compared with solving the original problem now." |
| 16 | "I do not believe coaching works." | They have seen vague advice sold as transformation. | "You should be skeptical. Good coaching is not motivation; it should change decisions, behavior, and measurable outcomes. What would prove it worked for you?" |
| 17 | "I do not have the budget for coaching." | They may want the outcome but cannot justify the spend yet. | "Then we should not force it. The question is whether staying where you are is cheaper than getting help changing it." |
| 18 | "The market is bad." | In real estate, they are using uncertainty to delay action. | "The market is not a reason by itself. It changes the strategy. Are you trying to avoid a bad move, or are you trying to avoid deciding?" |
| 19 | "I am just browsing." | They want control and no pressure. | "That is fine. Browsing gets useful when we define what would make something worth acting on. What would have to be true?" |
| 20 | "Not a priority right now." | The pain is real but not urgent enough. | "That may be true. What would have to happen for it to become a priority, and is any of that already starting?" |
| 21 | "We are happy with the status quo." | They have not connected the current process to a cost. | "Good. Then this should be quick. What would have to break for you to reconsider?" |
| 22 | "Your competitor is cheaper." | They are anchoring on price because differentiation is unclear. | "They may be. If price is the only difference, choose cheaper. The question is what you lose or gain with that choice." |

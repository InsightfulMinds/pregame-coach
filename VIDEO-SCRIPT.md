# The Pregame Coach — 55-Second Product Video

## Concept

Three-scene contrast: ChatGPT generic motivation vs. Pregame Coach specific coaching. Each scene shows the rep 5 minutes before a call. The point: generic advice may feel nice, but specific conversation prep gives the rep something usable.

---

## Scene 1: Generic Advice (0–18 seconds)

**Setting:** Rep at desk, phone in hand, nervous.

**ChatGPT Response (on screen):**
> "Be confident. Stay positive. Just be authentic. The key to sales is mindset."

**Rep's body language:** Stares at screen. Confused. Not less nervous. Scrolls for actual help. Time wasted.

**Voice-over (calm, direct):** "Generic motivation feels good for 3 seconds. Then you're alone with the call."

---

## Scene 2: Specific Coaching (19–40 seconds)

**Setting:** Same rep. Same desk. Same phone.

**Pregame Coach Prompt (on screen or Coach Rally dialogue):**
> Coach: "Top three things they'll say: 'I'm not interested,' 'Send me something,' or 'It's too expensive.' Here's what each one actually means and what you say back in one sentence."

**Display transitions:**
- Objection 1: "I'm not interested" → Psychology: "They haven't heard relevance yet" → Response: "Fair. Most people aren't interested before they know why I called. Give me 20 seconds."
- Objection 2: "Send me something" → Psychology: "They want an exit without conflict" → Response: "I can, but I don't want to dump generic info on you. What should I tailor it around?"
- Objection 3: "It's too expensive" → Psychology: "They don't see the value" → Response: "That's fair if it only feels like a cost. The question is whether the problem is already costing more than the fix."

**Coach Rally:** "Opening line: 'Hey, quick reason for my call: I help [specific group] solve [specific pain]. Is it off-base to ask if that's on your radar?' Energy check: how are you feeling from 1 to 10?"

**Rep's body language:** Sits up. Nods. Feels ready. Picks up phone.

**Voice-over:** "Specific prep removes the fog. Now you know what they'll say and what you'll say back."

---

## Scene 3: Side-by-Side Comparison (41–55 seconds)

**Setting:** Split screen. Left side is Scene 1 rep (still nervous, still looking for answers). Right side is Scene 2 rep (dialing with purpose).

**Text overlay:**

LEFT: "Generic advice"
- Sounds like confidence
- Feels good for 3 seconds
- Leaves you alone with the fear

RIGHT: "Specific prep"
- Predicts their objections
- You know what to say
- You dial with clarity

**Sound:** Left side = ambient anxiety noise (clock ticking, nervous breathing). Right side = dial tone, then "Hey, is [Name] available?"

**Final frame (53–55 seconds):** 
- Coach Rally logo
- Text: "The Pregame Coach"
- Subtext: "Warm up before the call."
- CTA: "Load the coach at [demo URL]"

## Production Notes

1. **Tone:** Direct, no music, no hype. Real rep, real moment, real coaching. Avoid cinematic clichés (motivational music, slow-mo, dramatic zooms).

2. **Rep casting:** Someone who looks like a real salesperson. Not a model. Gender-neutral preferred. Age 25–40.

3. **Pacing:** Speak at normal conversation speed. No rushing. Let silence work in Scene 1 to show confusion.

4. **Color palette:** Use the Pregame Coach brand colors (dark concrete #18191a, chalk cream #f0ebe0, gold accent #c49a3c, muted #6a6660). Keep it minimal.

5. **Graphics:**
   - Scene 2: Use cards or text boxes for each objection. Simple. Clean. Readable at speed.
   - Scene 3: Split screen is simple. No animation. Opacity fade between scenes.

6. **Voiceover:** Same person throughout (Coach Rally voice if possible, or neutral, calm authority). Not uplifting. Not urgent. Instructional.

7. **CTA:** Link to `/demo/index.html` or live demo URL when available.

8. **Length:** Exactly 55 seconds. Trim ruthlessly. Every frame moves the concept forward.

## Script Variant: Dialogue Version

If you want to use Coach Rally as a character in the video:

**Coach Rally (off-camera voice, Scene 2):**
> "You're calling a CFO about financial forecasting software. Frame it: you're here to create clarity, not force a yes. What will they say? Three things: 'I'm not interested,' 'Send me something,' or 'It's not urgent right now.' Here's what each one means and what you say back. Ready?"

**Rep nods.**

**Coach Rally:** "You have enough to start."

**Rep dials.**

This adds character and reinforces the brand voice without overcomplicating the visual.
